ABOUT TERRAIN FLUX

Terrain Flux is a SimCity 2000 style sandbox with real California terrain baked in. Sculpt hills, flood the coast, zone a town, and watch it grow. Or watch it refuse to grow, if you built it somewhere ridiculous.

Made by Jerrod Lessel, a geospatial scientist who wandered into GIS and never left. Part of Lessel Geospatial Labs.

Source code: GitHub

How it works

The map is a grid of 128 by 128 tiles, and each tile can tilt to make a slope. One rule keeps the land believable: the ground can only rise or fall one step from one tile to the next. When you raise or lower a spot, the land around it adjusts to follow that rule, which is what gives the hills their classic SimCity look.

Real places start as 30 m elevation data exported from Google Earth Engine. The app crops it to a square, shrinks it to fit the grid, and softens any cliffs too steep for the one-step rule. A city then grows on top using simple rules about roads, neighbors, and demand.

Debris flows after a fire use a simplified version of the USGS M1 likelihood model, the same one behind Scar Threshold, my real post-fire hazard map. Here the burn severity comes from the game's own fires and soil is held constant, so it is a toy, not a forecast.

CITY HAZARD REPORT
HOW THIS WORKS

The ground can only rise or fall one step from one tile to the next, so raising a spot drags the hillside up with it, just like the 1993 original.

📊 The report card grades your city on wildfire, debris flow, earthquake, sea level rise, air quality, and services, by the share of residents exposed. Click a row to see it on the map.

Factories, busy roads, and smoke pollute the air, and the wind carries it downwind. Homes grow slower in dirty air, so put industry downwind of town. Bus depots, EV charging, and bike share cut traffic pollution.

Zones only grow on flat tiles near a road. Use Level to make room. A lone zone stays small and rural. Taller buildings need a real neighborhood around them, and demand: homes need jobs nearby, and shops and factories need residents. Hover a zone to see what it is waiting for. Homes like water views and dislike dirty air.

Traffic follows development. Busy neighborhoods fill their roads with cars, and a road to nowhere stays empty. Every building type comes in four styles.

Civic buildings change the city around them. Parks make homes nicer, hospitals act like jobs, fire stations help factories, stadiums pack the shops and the roads, and every tower needs police coverage. Pick one to see its reach.

Wind gusts roll across the map and set the trees they pass swaying. Watch the wind readout in the status bar.

Sea level rise works on coastal real places. It floods using the real elevation, and only land connected to the ocean, so low spots behind high ground stay dry. Hover a flooded tile to see it, and the panel totals the land, buildings, and people affected.

Storms (🌧️) turn burn scars into debris flow danger. Each drainage gets a likelihood from a simplified version of the USGS M1 model used in Scar Threshold: steep burned ground, burn severity, and rain rate. Make it rain and High drainages usually let go, sending mud down the channel and spreading a fan where the ground flattens.

The ☀️ button switches between always day, a slow day and night cycle (one day is five real minutes, so nothing flashes), and always night. At night windows, streetlights, and headlights come on one by one.

Earthquakes (🌎): pick a magnitude and click an epicenter. Shaking fades with distance. Tall towers and heavy industry are the most fragile, steep slopes slide, low ground next to water can liquefy, bridges can drop, and collapsed buildings can start fires.

🔗 Share copies a link to exactly what you see, city and all. 📁 File saves or opens a full backup copy.

Drag a road across water to build a bridge. Bridges need land on both ends and can span up to 16 tiles.

Water pools on flat ground. Place it on a slope and it runs downhill as a stream, with waterfalls, until it reaches water or a low spot. The sea level buttons flood or drain the whole map.

Wildfires run downwind and uphill. Forest burns long and hot, grass burns fast and light, and roads, water, beaches, and bare rock stop them. Fire stations slow fires inside their reach. Burned ground stays scarred by severity and heals over about five game years, and forests grow back.

Load DEM takes a real GeoTIFF, crops the center square, and squeezes it into 128 by 128 tiles. Cliffs get softened into slopes because the engine refuses to do anything else.

Keys: Space pause, 1/2/3 slow/normal/fast, N day and night, Q/E rotate, +/- zoom, arrows or WASD pan, G grid, C contours, T tint, P pixels, Esc cancel.
